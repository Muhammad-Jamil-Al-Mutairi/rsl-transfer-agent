SECTION 1: ROSHN SAUDI LEAGUE SQUAD REGISTRATION & FOREIGN PLAYER
QUOTA RULES (2026 SEASON)
=====================================================================

=== Page 1: RSL Registration Rules ===

1.1 Squad Size
Each Roshn Saudi League (RSL) club must register a first-team squad of no fewer than
22 and no more than 30 professional players ahead of the season deadline. Clubs must
submit two registration lists per season: the Summer List (closes at the end of the
summer transfer window) and the Winter List (closes at the end of the January window).
A club fielding an ineligible player in a competitive fixture forfeits the match 3-0
under RSL Disciplinary Code Article 14.

1.2 Foreign Player Quota — "8+2" Rule
The League Registration Committee enforces an "8+2" foreign player quota for every
club competing in the Roshn Saudi League:
  - A maximum of EIGHT (8) senior foreign players may be registered and listed on the
    official matchday squad sheet at any one time.
  - An ADDITIONAL maximum of TWO (2) foreign players may be registered exclusively
    under the Under-21 Foreign Development Slot (see Section 1, Page 2), separate
    from and in addition to the 8 senior foreign slots.
  - This means the absolute ceiling on foreign players registered by a single club is
    TEN (10): 8 senior + 2 U21 development slots.
  - There is no maximum limit on the number of Saudi national players a club may
    register, subject to the overall squad-size ceiling of 30 professionals.

1.3 Enforcement
Any club found to have registered more than 8 senior foreign players, or more than 2
U21 foreign development players, will have the registration of the excess player(s)
voided by the Committee and will be referred to the RSL Financial & Regulatory Panel
for a mandatory compliance review. Clubs are strongly advised to run a compliance
check (via the squad registration validator) before submitting any new signing for
official registration.

=== Page 2: Foreign Player Quotas & U21 Development Slot Rules ===

2.1 Definition of "Foreign Player"
A player is classified as "foreign" for RSL registration purposes if they do not hold
Saudi Arabian nationality and are not eligible for a Saudi passport under the
Ministry of Sport's naturalized-player pathway at the time of registration.

2.2 Under-21 (U21) Foreign Development Slot — Eligibility
To qualify for one of the two dedicated U21 Foreign Development Slots (separate from
the 8 senior foreign slots), a foreign player MUST have been born on or after
1 January 2005 for the 2026 season (i.e., aged 21 or under on 1 July 2026). Clubs
must submit birth-certificate or passport verification to the Registration Committee
before a player can be classified into a U21 slot rather than a senior slot.

2.3 Slot Flexibility
A U21-eligible foreign player (born 2005 or later) MAY be voluntarily registered
against a senior foreign slot instead of a U21 slot if the club has already filled
both U21 slots — this is permitted and does not violate the quota, since it simply
uses one of the 8 senior allocations. However, a foreign player born before 1 January
2005 can NEVER be registered against a U21 slot, only against a senior slot.

2.4 Homegrown Ratio Guideline
As a football-development guideline (not a hard registration block, but monitored by
the League's Sporting Development Unit), RSL recommends that at least 50% of a
club's registered senior squad consists of Saudi national ("homegrown") players.
Clubs falling below this ratio for two consecutive seasons may be required to submit
a Player Development Improvement Plan to the League.

SECTION 2: FINANCIAL SUSTAINABILITY & SALARY CAP GUIDELINES FOR
RSL CLUBS
=====================================================================

=== Page 3: FFP Overview & Salary Cap Mechanism ===

3.1 Purpose
The RSL Financial Fair Play (FFP) & Sustainability Framework exists to ensure that
clubs do not accumulate transfer and wage spending that outstrips sustainable
football-related revenue, preserving long-term competitive balance across the League.

3.2 Squad Cost Control (SCC) Ratio
Each club's aggregate spend on wages, transfer amortization, and agent fees must not
exceed 85% of the club's total adjusted football revenue (broadcast distribution,
matchday income, sponsorship, and commercial revenue), calculated on a rolling
three-season average. This is known as the Squad Cost Control (SCC) Ratio.

3.3 Reporting Currency
All FFP submissions to the League's Club Licensing & Financial Sustainability Panel
must be reported in Saudi Riyal (SAR). Transfer fees and salaries agreed in a foreign
currency (EUR, GBP, USD) must be converted to SAR at the League's official quarterly
reference rate at the time of the transaction for reporting purposes. The reference
rates currently in effect are: 1 EUR = 4.08 SAR, 1 GBP = 4.78 SAR, 1 USD = 3.75 SAR.

3.4 Materiality Threshold
Any single transfer fee or signing-on package exceeding SAR 75,000,000 (approximately
EUR 18.4M at the reference rate) must be individually disclosed and pre-cleared by
the Financial Sustainability Panel before the player can be officially registered.

=== Page 4: Penalties for FFP Non-Compliance ===

4.1 Tiered Sanctions
Clubs that breach the 85% Squad Cost Control Ratio face a tiered sanctions regime:
  - Breach of up to 5 percentage points over the threshold (i.e., up to 90%): a
    formal written warning and a mandatory remediation plan within 90 days.
  - Breach of 5-15 percentage points over the threshold (90%-100%): a transfer levy
    of 10% on the club's next two windows of incoming transfer spend, remitted to
    the League's Solidarity Fund.
  - Breach of more than 15 percentage points over the threshold (above 100%): a
    squad registration freeze — the club may not register any new signing, foreign
    or domestic, until the following registration window, plus a fine of up to
    SAR 20,000,000.

4.2 Appeals
Clubs may appeal a sanction to the independent RSL Financial Appeals Board within 14
days of notification. Appeals do not suspend a registration freeze while pending.

4.3 Advisory Note
Clubs are strongly encouraged to model the SAR-equivalent cost of any prospective
transfer (fee + estimated first-year wages) against their current SCC Ratio headroom
before entering formal negotiations, to avoid triggering an unplanned sanction.
